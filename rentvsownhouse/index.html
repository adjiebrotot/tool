What this assumes

    Questions

    What does the renter do with the money they don't spend on a home?

    The renter saves it at the Savings Rate, compounded monthly and untaxed. Both scenarios start with the same cash (the deposit plus setup cost, so $192,000 at the starting values), and with the monthly housing budget left blank each year's budget is the dearer scenario's cost, so the cheaper one saves the gap. Raising the Savings Rate from 3% to 6% lifts the renter's year-30 net equity from about $756,000 to $1.70 million, while the owner's moves only from $3.62 million to $3.66 million.

    How is net equity worked out for someone who owns?

    It is the home's value less Selling cost, less the loan still owed, plus savings, as if the home were sold at the end of each year. No tax is taken on the gain. Selling cost matters to the timing: at the starting values over 10 years, 0% selling cost puts the breakeven in year 2, 2.5% in year 3 and 5% in year 4.

    What does the breakeven year mean, and why is there sometimes none?

    It is the first year from which owning stays ahead of renting until the end of the Time horizon. The search runs backwards from the final year, so a lead that is lost again later is not counted, and if renting is ahead in the last year there is no breakeven. At the starting values, House price growth of 2% a year gives year 15, 3% gives year 7 and 5% gives year 3.

    Does the model include tax on rental savings or on the sale of the home?

    No. Savings interest is untaxed, no capital gains tax is taken when the home is sold, and there is no tax relief on the loan. The model also has no investment return other than the Savings Rate. To allow for tax, enter an after-tax savings rate as the Savings Rate, add any seller tax to Selling cost, and put land tax or similar into Ongoing costs of owning.

    How does the model treat a fixed rate that later turns floating?

    Set the mortgage to Detail mode and give each period of the Mortgage rate schedule a fixed rate or a floating range. Floating periods run at the middle of their range, and the chart band shows the low and high ends. Each change of rate re-spreads the balance over the term left, so the payment is recalculated. The Jakarta Quick Start, as of October 2026, uses 8.5% fixed for 5 years, then floating at 11% to 13% (so 12% in the table).

    How does Rent-Then-Buy price the later purchase?

    It buys at the home's price grown at House price growth to the end of the chosen year, with the same Down payment, setup costs scaled to that price and a new loan on the same terms. At the starting values, a purchase after 5 years costs about $1.02 million instead of $800,000. The cash it saved while renting becomes that deposit, and the model warns if the starting cash falls short.
