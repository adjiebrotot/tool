How to use the DCA Scenario Explorer

Put a set amount into an asset on a schedule, or when a signal fires, and see what it would have grown to. Part 1 shows how to use the tool and its Portfolio mode. Part 2 shows how every figure is worked out. Every demo is the real tool, running live in your browser. Press Pause on one to try it yourself.

Open the tool Skip to how the numbers work

The short version

  1. 1Load the prices

    One click on a Quick Start loads an example. Or list tickers on the Data tab, or make up an asset from a return and a volatility, and set the Date Range.

  2. 2Set each scenario

    On Scenarios, each plan gets an asset, an Amount per top-up and an Investment Style: a fixed day, a price move, a technical signal, or a hindsight benchmark.

  3. 3Simulate and read

    Press ▶ Simulate. One sentence names the best plan; the Final Summary gives each one's value and ROI. Portfolio mode does the same for whole portfolios.

Nothing is uploaded. Prices come from Yahoo Finance through the site's own server and are kept in your browser; every run is worked out there. This guide is general information, not financial advice.

The screen

Inputs on the left, the answer and the charts beside them, the detail under the charts. Point at a part in the list to see where it is.

  1. Inputs

    Quick Start, save and open, then three tabs: Data, Scenarios and Assumptions. ▶ Simulate sits at the bottom. The chevrons fold the card to a slim Inputs rail.

  2. The answer

    One sentence: which scenario did best, and by how much. Under it, the Final Summary: a tile per scenario, and Advanced metrics.

  3. Charts

    Security Prices, with each buy marked if you like, and Portfolio Value Over Time against the money put in. Each has Share and ⟳.

  4. Details

    The Detailed Breakdown, every buy of one scenario, and What this assumes, both opening from their chevrons.

Portfolio mode, linked above the inputs as the Portfolio DCA Simulator, runs whole portfolios: several assets at target weights, rebalanced, with fees and a cash account.

  1. Inputs

    The same Data and Assumptions tabs. Portfolios holds a tab per portfolio, each with Top-Ups, Assets and Rebalancing.

  2. The answer

    Which portfolio ended highest, then a tile per portfolio: value, net gain, ROI, top-ups and the share left in cash.

  3. Portfolio Value Over Time

    A line per portfolio against the money topped up. In Optimise mode it becomes the sweep.

  4. Detailed Breakdown

    One portfolio at a time: its assets' prices, its Composition Over Time, and the Breakdown Table of every event.

Top right on both pages, Tour replays a one minute walk through the page, and Guide opens this page. The ticker reference lists the codes Yahoo Finance uses for shares, funds, indices, currencies and crypto.

Quick Start

Three worked comparisons on real prices: Monthly vs Weekly ($520 a month against $120 a week into SPY), Equity vs Money Market ($500 a month into DHHF.AX against AAA.AX) and Stock vs ETF ($500 a month into AAPL against SPY). One click replaces the scenarios and runs them.

On a phone, press Full screen under a demo and turn the phone sideways to read it.

  1. Open the inputs

    On a first visit the card is folded to a rail, so the answer has the room. Click Inputs to open it; it stays the way you leave it.

  2. Pick an example

    Hover a button to read what it compares. A click loads it and runs it.

  3. Read the answer

    $520 a month and $120 a week put in about the same money, so the sentence compares the return on it: 56.68% against 55.87%.

  • Every demo here runs 1 Oct 2021 to 30 Sep 2026, on prices saved with this guide. The tool opens on the last five years to today, so your own run of an example will differ.
  • A Quick Start is a fresh start. It clears the scenarios and settings first; prices already loaded stay, so no download is wasted.

The Data tab

What every scenario can buy, and the days it runs over. Load prices once here; scenarios then pick from them.

  1. Currency

    Currency Symbol is the label money wears. Nothing is converted.

  2. Loaded assets

    Folded to one line. Open it to see each ticker's name and dates, remove one, Refresh to today or Clear all.

  3. Real or Simulated

    Real: list Tickers and press ⤓ Load tickers. Simulated: an Asset name, a Return and a Standard deviation, then + Add Simulated Asset.

  4. Date Range

    Start and End. The run uses the trading days every scenario's asset shares.

  • Five loads a day, 25 tickers each. The data server is shared, so list everything at once. A ticker already loaded costs nothing, and both pages share the same prices.
  • Prices from 1990 on. A load fetches each ticker's whole history from 1 Jan 1990, so changing the dates within it needs no new load.

The Scenarios tab

A scenario is one plan: an asset, how much each top-up puts in, and when it buys. Add as many as you like; each runs on its own and they are compared.

  1. A tab per scenario

    Click a tab to edit it, double-click to rename it. + adds one, the icons copy or remove the one open.

  2. Pick the asset

    Asset lists the loaded tickers and simulated assets. Type a ticker or part of its name.

  3. Name it

    The name and the colour beside it are labels only: changing them never sends the results out of date.

  4. Top-ups

    Amount per top-up, then Yearly increase: 10 makes year 2 +10% and year 3 +21%.

  5. Simulate

    Any change greys the results and lights ▶ Simulate, so a chart never shows numbers the form no longer holds.

  • The amount is per purchase. A weekly style buys 52 times a year, a monthly one 12. Switching between them restates the amount so a year's money stays the same: $500 a month becomes $115.38 a week.

Investment Style

When a scenario buys. Four families, each opened by its pill: only the options of the open family, and the settings of the style picked, show.

  1. Date

    Monthly (Fixed Date) on a Day of month, or Weekly (Fixed Day) on a Day of week. A holiday moves it to the closest trading day.

  2. Momentum

    Buy the Dip or Buy the Peak: once the price has moved the Threshold from the period's first close. Invest at End of Month if target not reached buys anyway on its last day.

  3. Technical

    MA Crossover, RSI Oversold Buy, Bollinger Band Dip, MACD Bullish Cross, MACD Histogram Recovery, ADX Trend Filter, each with its own settings. At most one buy a month or a week, by Frequency.

  4. Forward

    The month's or week's highest or lowest price day. They need tomorrow's prices, so they are benchmarks: the best and worst a plan on that cadence could have done.

  5. Run it

    Show Indicators draws the signal behind a technical style, Show Buy Date marks each buy under the price.

  • A signal that never fires buys nothing. With the end-of-period box off, a month without a signal is skipped, so less money goes in. Compare such a plan on ROI, not on its final value.
  • Indicators start from the first date. They are worked out on the prices in the Date Range alone, so a 200-day average gives no signal in the first 200 trading days.

The Assumptions tab

Three settings every scenario shares.

  1. Trade at

    Every rule decides on a day's close. The order fills at Same day's close, Next day's open (the default: decide in the evening, buy next morning) or Next day's close.

  2. Risk-Free Rate

    What money earns without risk, a year. It only feeds Sharpe and Sortino and moves no money.

  3. Simulation Seed

    Fixes the random path of a simulated asset, so one seed always gives the same prices.

  4. Simulate

    A fill at the close here moves the result by about $24 on $48,884: when you buy within a day hardly matters.

The answer and the charts

The sentence at the top is the whole answer. Under it, the figures behind it and two charts.

  1. The answer

    The same money in: the highest final value wins. Different money in: the highest return on it does.

  2. Final Summary

    Each tile: the final value, ROI, Topped up and Trades. Advanced metrics adds Sharpe, Sortino, CAGR (TWR) and CAGR (MWR).

  3. Security Prices

    The price of the first scenario's asset; click a legend entry to add another, scaled to 100 so they compare. Candlesticks shows each day's open, high, low and close.

  4. Portfolio Value

    What each scenario holds, day by day, over the dashed Total deposited. The gap between them is the gain.

  • Blue and gold are options, not good and bad. Scenarios take the site's neutral colours; red is kept for a price falling.
  • Zoom and pan a chart with the wheel or a drag; ⟳ resets it. Hover to read every scenario on that day.

Detailed Breakdown and What this assumes

Under the charts, the working behind the answer.

  1. Detailed Breakdown

    Opens from its chevron: a row per buy with the day it filled, the day it was Decided, the price, the units, the running totals and the return so far.

  2. Pick a scenario

    The menu picks whose buys show. ⬇ CSV saves them.

  3. What this assumes

    The figures and rules this run used, from your own inputs: the price source, the dates, the fills, the top-ups.

Optimise

The gold gauge beside a setting sweeps it: the scenario on screen runs once for every value in a range, up to two settings at once, so you see how the answer moves and where it peaks.

  1. Pick a setting

    Click the gauge beside it. Any amount, day, threshold or indicator setting has one.

  2. Set the range

    Start value, End value and Steps, or a pick from a list. The line under it counts the runs.

  3. Optimise

    The button reads ▶ Optimise and Cancel sits beside it. Only this scenario runs, so its tabs stay locked until you cancel.

  4. Read the sweep

    The answer names the best value against yours; the tiles show Best, Your current setting and Worst. Two settings draw a surface.

  5. Show a run below

    Shown in the charts below picks the tested value the charts and the breakdown show. A click on the chart does the same.

  • Show picks what the sweep ranks by: ROI, Final value, Net, Sharpe, Sortino, CAGR (TWR) or CAGR (MWR).
  • Up to 625 runs. 50 values for one setting, or 25 by 25 for two.
  • The best is the best for these past prices, not a forecast. Here every day from the 1st to the 28th lands within a few hundred dollars.

Save, export and share

The tool keeps your scenarios in this browser between visits. The rest makes something to keep or send.

  1. Save or open a file

    The two icons beside Quick Start write every scenario and setting to a file, and read one back. Prices are not saved; a load fetches them again.

  2. Share a chart

    As SVG or PNG, a copy to the clipboard, a link that opens your scenarios, or an embed for a web page.

  • An embed carries its own prices, just the dates it draws, so it shows the same chart on any site without loading anything. A link opens the tool on your scenarios and loads prices as a visit does.

Part 2

How the numbers work

What each field means, where it enters the sums, and what moving it does to the answer and why. All of it is read from the tool's own code (script.js on each page, the indicators and fills in shared.js).

The model in one picture

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means putting a set amount in on a schedule, whatever the price. Each top-up buys as many units as its money buys that day, in fractions where needed. The holding is worth its units at each day's close. At the end the tool asks two things: what is it worth, and what did it return on the money put in?

Units bought ui = aipieach top-up
Final value V = U × Pend, with U = Σ ui
ROI = V − DD = Pendaverage cost − 1average cost = D ÷ U
  • aithe money in top-up i
  • pithe price it fills at (Trade at)
  • Uunits held at the end
  • Pendthe close on the last day
  • DTopped up: the sum of every ai

The last form is the whole of DCA in one line: your return is the last price over the average price you paid. And a fixed amount pays less than the average price, because it buys more units when the price is low and fewer when it is high. With equal top-ups the average cost is the harmonic mean of the fill prices, which is never above their ordinary mean:

Average cost = n × aΣ a ÷ pi = nΣ 1 ÷ pi ≤ Σ pin
  • In the example below, SPY's 60 fills averaged $512.90, but $520 a month paid $486.74 a unit on average: 5% less, from buying more in the 2022 fall.
  • The answer compares like with like. When every scenario put in about the same money (within 0.5%), the highest final value wins. When they did not, more money in is not a better plan, so the sentence ranks them by ROI.
  • DCA against a lump sum. The tool has no lump-sum scenario. A lump sum puts all the money to work on day one; a monthly plan has, on average, about half of it at work. In a market that rose, as SPY did (from $406 to $763 over the window, up 88%), the lump sum ends ahead. What DCA buys is protection from putting everything in at a peak. CAGR (TWR), below, is the nearest stand-in for the lump sum's rate.

The prices include dividends. Yahoo Finance's adjusted close folds every dividend and split into the price, so a holding grows as if dividends were reinvested. A ticker that falls back to Stooq is unadjusted: it shows ⚠️ in the loaded list, and What this assumes says its dividends are left out.

Data: the prices and the dates

  • σStandard deviation, a year
  • zta random draw, close to a standard normal (the sum of three uniform draws)

The figures: ROI, CAGR (TWR), CAGR (MWR), Sharpe and Sortino

ROI is the plain gain on what went in. The rest come from the daily value, starting on the first day money is at work, and turn the result into a rate a year and a reward for the risk taken.

Daily return rt = Vt − FtVt−1 − 1the day's top-up taken out
CAGR (TWR) = (Π (1 + rt))1 ÷ Y − 1
CAGR (MWR): the rate x with Σ ai(1 + x)ti = V(1 + x)Tthe internal rate of return (XIRR)
Sharpe = mean(r − rf)sd(r) × √252Sortino: the same over the downside deviation, √mean(min(0, r − rf)²)

  • Yyears from the first buy to the last day
  • ti, Tyears from the first buy to top-up i, and to the end
  • rfthe daily risk-free rate, (1 + Rf)1÷252 − 1

Watch out: no drawdown, and Sharpe is daily noise. The tool does not report the deepest fall. And with daily returns a few large days dominate Sharpe and Sortino; over five years they rank plans, they do not promise the next five.

  • How return and risk combine. On a day with no trades, the portfolio's return is its holdings' returns weighted by their value: rp = Σ wkrk, cash at its rate. Its swing is less than the weighted swings whenever the assets do not move together. The tool measures that from the portfolio's own daily values, so the benefit shows up in its Sharpe.
  • 80/20 against 60/40. More in shares earned more: $78,915 against $75,604 on $60,000, CAGR (TWR) 8.42% against 7.19%. But 60/40 had the higher Sharpe, 1.08 against 0.96: per unit of swing, the cash-heavy mix paid better.
  • Rebalancing costs and helps. Over these five years, splitting each top-up 50/50 and never rebalancing ended $1,720 ahead of keeping SPY and GOVT at 50/50: shares beat bonds, and the unrebalanced mix let the winner grow.

Watch out: waiting cash earns nothing unless you set a rate. The Risk-free rate starts at 0%. A quarterly rebalance or a rare trigger can leave real money idle; the tile's Cash share and the table's Interest show how much.

What moves the answer

Each input moved on its own from the worked example, on the tool itself, with the prices this guide ships.

  • When you start, and how much you put in, are the answer. A year either way on the start date moves the result by $12,000 to $13,000: more top-ups, and a different stretch of market. The amount moves it in proportion.
  • Timing within the month hardly matters. The 10th or the 20th instead of the 1st: under $200. A fill at the close instead of the open: $24. Even perfect hindsight, buying every month at its lowest close, adds only $1,321.
  • ROI tells a different story from value. Starting a year earlier lifted ROI from 56.68% to 65.68%, starting later cut it to 48.62%; the amount left it unchanged; a 5% yearly increase put $3,280 more in but cut ROI to 54.50%, because later money had less time to grow.
  • So spend your effort on the plan, not the day. Choose an asset and an amount you can keep up through a fall; a clever buy day is worth a rounding error by comparison.

What it leaves out

What the answer rests on, beyond the figures you enter. What this assumes, under the results, lists the ones your run uses.

  • The past only. Every real run replays prices that already happened. A plan that won over five years can lose over the next five.
  • No tax, and no fees on the main page. Portfolio mode charges a % fee on every trade; neither page has tax, brokerage minimums or spreads.
  • One currency, never converted. The symbol is a label; mixing an ASX and a US ticker adds Australian and US dollars as if they were one.
  • Dividends ride in the adjusted price, as if reinvested on the day, with no tax or delay. A Stooq series leaves them out.
  • Fractional units at the fill price. Every top-up buys its whole amount at the open or close, whatever its size.
  • Nothing is sold on the main page, and the holding is valued, not sold, on the last day.
  • A simulated asset is one random path with a constant return and volatility: a what-if, not a forecast. Change the seed to see another.
  • Indicators see only the Date Range. A long average needs months of the window to warm up before it can signal.
  • No drawdown or worst-case figure. Read the Portfolio Value chart for the falls.

Do not lean on it when the best plan topped up a different amount from yours, a winning style is a Forward one, a sweep's best value sits alone on a spike, or your money is in a different currency from the ticker.
