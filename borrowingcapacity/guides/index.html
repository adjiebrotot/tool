Income

What you earn, and how much of it a lender counts. Simple takes one gross figure a year, counted in full. Detailed asks stream by stream, each with its Shading: the share a lender counts.

  1. Simple or Detailed

    The Detail switch. Finance Bro opens Detailed: $230,000 base salary at 100%, a $90,000 bonus at 80%.

  2. Shade each stream

    The box beside each amount. A lender taking only half the bonus cuts capacity from $906,340 to $624,187, while the tax stays on all of it.

  3. Anything else you receive

    Tick Rent, dividends or an investment property or Family Tax Benefit, pension or child support and their fields appear.

  • I earn as. Employee or Self-employed picks which side of the form counts. Self-employed asks for the two year average profit after tax (NPAT) and add-backs.
  • An unticked box means it does not exist. Its fields are zeroed, so a figure left in a hidden group never leaks into the answer.
  • Simple and Detailed hand the figure across. Detailed opens on streams that add up to the Simple figure, base salary taking the difference, and Simple reopens on what the streams add up to.

Household

Who is applying, who depends on you and where you live. It sets the tax, through two taxpayers or one, and the living costs a lender assumes, through the HEM benchmark.

  1. Who is applying

    Adults in the household, then the Second applicant's share of income. At 0% the couple's $185,000 is taxed in one name: $12,980 more tax a year and $135,643 less capacity.

  2. Living costs and HEM

    Under Declared living expenses a note says whether HEM is used instead. Declare $6,000 a month, above HEM, and it counts: capacity falls to $601,092.

  3. Tax and study loan

    Resident tax year sets the lowest bracket. A study loan is deducted as tax or counted as a commitment; the same dollars either way.

  • City scales HEM by local living costs without housing, Perth at 100.
  • Private hospital cover. Off, and the Medicare levy surcharge applies once income passes its threshold.

Debts

What you already owe and will keep paying. Each repayment comes out of the income the new loan could use; each balance counts against the debt-to-income cap.

  1. Tick what you have

    Each box opens its group. A $600 a month car loan takes capacity from $416,250 to $341,009.

  2. A card counts at its limit

    Credit card and line of credit limits: the limit, not the balance. Closing the $8,000 card lifts capacity to $379,131.

  3. Detailed

    Shows the Assessed monthly rate on cards (3.0% to 3.8%) and the term and type of other home loans.

  4. After settlement

    Occupancy, any rent you keep paying, and the Strata, rates and insurance on the new home.

  • Other home and investment loans are reassessed at the higher of their rate plus the buffer and the lender floor, as principal and interest over the term left.
  • Closing at settlement. A facility you pay out at settlement comes off commitments, and its balance off the debt-to-income cap.

Loan

The rate you are offered and the term. A lender tests you at a higher rate, so the note under the fields says what it is and what else it insists on.

  1. Rate and term

    Product rate offered and Loan term. At 6.40% instead of 5.90% the couple is tested at 9.40% and capacity falls to $657,222.

  2. Interest only to start

    The test runs over the years left after it ends. Five years of it take capacity to $632,057.

  3. Lender policy

    Detailed shows the APRA buffer, the Lender floor, Minimum left each month (UMI) and Minimum income cover (NSR).

  • The assessment rate is the higher of the product rate plus the buffer (3.00%) and the floor (5.50%).

Purchase

The home, the deposit and the costs of buying it. This tab sets three of the four caps: debt to income, loan to value and the deposit.

  1. Price, deposit and costs

    Purchase price, Deposit funds, Stamp duty and Other purchase costs. The note under the deposit says what is left after costs.

  2. Lenders Mortgage Insurance

    On, the lender goes to 95% of the price. Off, it stops at 80%, the $66,000 deposit binds, and capacity falls from $426,671 to $264,000.

  3. Detailed: LVR and DTI

    Maximum LVR, the DTI cap and its Income basis, for a lender stricter than the usual.

  • An amount or a percentage. Deposit funds as an amount is cash in the bank, with stamp duty and costs paid out of it first. As a percentage of the price it is the down payment, with the costs on top. Switching restates the figure, so the answer stays put.
  • Bank valuation (Detailed). The loan to value cap uses the lower of price and valuation.

  1. The answer

    What a lender would likely lend, whether that buys the home you entered, and which cap sets the limit.

  2. Six figures

    Binding constraint names the cap and how far the next one sits above it. NSR and UMI say how tight it is.

  3. Every cap across incomes

    Hover to read all four at any income. Your capacity is the lowest line at each point.

  4. The four caps

    Each limit, its headroom above the answer, and which one binds.

  5. The working

    Serviceability build-up runs from gross income to the loan, line by line. Its chevron opens it.

  • Max purchase price is the capacity plus the deposit left after costs. In percentage duty mode it reads a little low: enter it as the price to converge.
  • Warnings sit at the foot of the inputs: a surplus that covers no repayment, an LVR above 80% without LMI, a debt-to-income ratio past 6, an NSR under its minimum.
  • What this assumes lists every figure the answer rests on, in words, for the scenario on screen.

Part 2

How the numbers work

How an Australian lender sizes a home loan, as this tool does it: every field, where it enters the sums, and what moving it does to the answer.

The model in one picture

A lender asks four separate questions and lends the smallest answer. Can your income repay it? Is the total debt a sane multiple of income? Is the loan a safe share of the property? Does your deposit reach that share?

Borrowing capacity C = min( Lserv, LDTI, LLVR, Ldep )
Lserv = R × 1 − (1 + i)−ni ÷ (1 + m)serviceability
R = N − E − K − Uthe largest new repayment, a month
  • Nnet income a month: counted income less tax
  • Eliving costs: the higher of declared and HEM
  • Kexisting commitments a month
  • Uthe minimum left each month (UMI)
  • ithe assessment rate ÷ 12
  • nmonths tested: the term, less any interest only
  • mthe LMI premium, when it is capitalised
  • LDTI, LLVR, Ldepthe three hard caps (Purchase)

Serviceability turns a monthly surplus into a loan: the present value of the repayment over the term, at the rate the lender tests you at. At 8.90% over 30 years every $1 a month supports $125.40 of loan, so every $100 a month of commitments costs about $12,540 of capacity.

  • The deposit binds when it is thin. The first home buyer's $66,000 deposit, after costs, supports $264,000 at 80% LVR (four dollars of loan for each dollar down) but $1,254,000 at 95% (nineteen). LMI is what lets the income decide.

  • Debt to income binds only for a stricter lender. Set the couple's DTI cap to 3.5 and it does, at $632,500.

Household: living costs and the tax scale

A lender does not trust a low expense figure. It takes the higher of what you declare and a benchmark for a household like yours, the Household Expenditure Measure (HEM). This tool builds a HEM-style benchmark from household size, income and city.

HEM = ( b + $480 × d ) × c100 × fa month
Living costs E = max( declared, HEM )
  • b$1,850 for one applicant, $2,750 for two
  • ddependants
  • cthe city index, Perth = 100
  • fthe income band on total gross income: up to $60,000 ×1.00, $90,000 ×1.08, $130,000 ×1.18, $180,000 ×1.30, $250,000 ×1.45, above ×1.62
  • Income tax runs once per applicant on their share of taxable income. 2026-27: nil to $18,200, 15% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000, 45% above. The lowest rate is 16% in 2025-26 and 14% from 2027-28.
  • LITO is $700, less 5c a dollar above $37,500 and 1.5c above $45,000, gone by $66,667.
  • Medicare levy is 2% of combined taxable income: nil up to $27,222 single or $45,907 for a family (plus $4,216 a dependant), phased in at 10c a dollar to 1.25 times that.
  • Medicare levy surcharge, without hospital cover: 1%, 1.25% or 1.5% above $101,000, $118,000 or $158,000 single; $202,000, $236,000 or $316,000 for a family, plus $1,500 for each child after the first.
  • HELP repays 15% of the higher earner's repayment income above the threshold up to $125,000, and 17% above.

Declaring less than HEM gains nothing. The couple declare $4,800 a month, but HEM for two adults and two children in Melbourne on $185,000 is $5,315, so $5,315 is used. Cutting the declared figure changes nothing; only a figure above HEM moves the answer.

  • Two taxpayers, not one. Pooling $185,000 in one name pushes it into the 37% bracket. Split 50/50 the couple pay $40,240 a year; all in one name, $53,220.
  • A raise can lower capacity. HEM steps up at each band edge. For the couple, $181,000 of income supports $57,469 less than $180,000, because HEM jumps from $4,765 to $5,315 a month. The dips in the chart's serviceability line are these steps.

Loan: the rate you are tested at

You may pay 5.90%, but APRA requires lenders to test that you could pay at least 3 points more, and many set a floor too. The test rate, not the rate you pay, turns the surplus into a loan.

  • Rate and buffer are one number to the test. One point on either moves the answer by the same amount, because only their sum counts. The floor matters only when it is higher, which at 5.50% it is not.
  • What you would really pay. The couple's capacity costs $5,478 a month at 8.90%, the test, and $4,075 at the 5.90% they would pay. The gap is the buffer for rate rises.

Capacity is the loan on this home. The LVR cap is a share of the price you enter, so a cheaper home can lower the answer. For the couple, a $828,000 home caps the loan at $662,400, below what their income services.

  • A deposit is leverage. At 80% LVR each dollar of deposit supports four of loan; at 95%, nineteen. That is why LMI turns the first home buyer's $66,000 from the binding cap into one with room to spare.
  • LMI cuts both ways. It lifts the LVR and deposit caps, but the premium is borrowed too, so the serviceability cap is divided by 1.025. On a thin margin it can leave a borrower worse off.

What moves it most

Each bar lowers or raises one input by a step worth arguing about, and keeps the rest of the Couple, Melbourne example where it was.

  • The downside is all serviceability. A third child, $1,000 a month more declared spending or a 1 point higher rate each take $57,000 to $86,000 off. Those are the risks to price in before you bid.
  • The upside hits a wall. Without the LVR cap, 10% more income would add $131,000 and a 1 point lower rate $67,000. On a $920,000 home neither can push the loan past $736,000; to borrow more the couple would need a dearer home, or LMI.
  • The cheap levers. Expenses below HEM and a bigger deposit do nothing here. Cutting the card limit is the one change in their own hands that lifts the answer.

  1. One more dependant

    HEM rises $688 a month and capacity falls to $600,767. The serviceability line drops across the chart.

  2. One fewer

    HEM falls below the $4,800 declared, so the declared figure is used. Capacity rises to $736,000 and Binding constraint turns to loan to value.

  3. Back to two

    Serviceability binds again at $686,999.

What it leaves out

A model of the common approach, not any one bank's credit policy. Shading, HEM tables, floors, card rates and caps all differ between lenders.

  • HEM is a stand-in. The real benchmark is a licensed table from the Melbourne Institute. Here it is $1,850 or $2,750 plus $480 a dependant at Perth prices, six income bands and a city index.
  • Tax is the resident scale only. LITO and the Medicare levy, with no other deductions, offsets or salary sacrifice. The levy and the surcharge run on combined income; HELP is charged on the higher earner's share, and negative gearing uses that earner's rate.
  • Purchase costs are yours to enter. Stamp duty is not worked out by state, and first home grants and concessions are not applied.
  • LMI is a flat percentage. Real premiums climb steeply with LVR and loan size. The LVR and deposit caps apply to the loan before the premium is added.
  • The NSR minimum never resizes the loan. It is a pass or fail check; UMI alone sizes serviceability.
  • The new home's own rent is not counted. An investor's purchase earns nothing unless you add the rent under Investments.
  • Rates stand still. One rate for the whole term: no fixed period ending, no offset account, no rate change.
  • A lender weighs more. Credit history, how long you have held the job, genuine savings, the property type and postcode, and your age against the term.

When not to lean on it. Complex self-employment, trusts or overseas income; a lender you know is stricter; or a next cap within a few per cent of the binding one, where a small change flips which cap decides. Then ask a broker or the lender.
