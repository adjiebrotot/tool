The four caps

Each cap is the largest loan it allows, in dollars. Your borrowing capacity is the smallest of the four.

How serviceability is worked out

Dollars a month and a year. Amounts taken off income carry a minus sign.

Income a lender counts (shading)

Dollars a year. Each stream counts at its share, so the counted figure can sit below what you earn.

Monthly commitments

Dollars a month, as the lender assesses them. A facility closing at settlement carries a minus sign.

What this assumes

  • One common lender approach, not any bank's policy. Shading, HEM, the buffer, the floor and the caps all differ between lenders, so treat the answer as a guide, not a pre-approval.
  • Income counts at its share. Simple counts one gross figure in full; Detailed counts each stream at the share you set.
  • Tax is the resident scale for the year you pick, with LITO, the Medicare levy, the surcharge without private hospital cover, and the study loan when you deduct it as tax. Two applicants are taxed as two people on the income split.
  • Living costs are the higher of what you declare and HEM for your household, income band and city.
  • Other loans are reassessed at the higher of their rate plus the buffer and the lender floor, over the term left. Cards and lines of credit count on the limit, not the balance.
  • The new loan is tested at the higher of your rate plus the APRA buffer and the lender floor, as principal and interest over the term less any interest only period, keeping the minimum left each month (UMI).
  • Capacity is the lowest of four caps: serviceability, debt to income, loan to value and deposit. The most you could pay is that loan plus your deposit, after stamp duty and costs when the deposit is entered as an amount.
  • Stamp duty is the share or amount you enter. State concessions and first home grants are not worked out for you.
