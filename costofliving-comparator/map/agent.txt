Cost of Living Comparator: Map View: use this tool with an AI assistant

For people: Ask an AI assistant that can read web pages (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and
others) to use this page for your question. It replies with a link that opens this tool on your
scenario and lists what it assumed, and the tool works out the answer. The rest of this section is
written for the assistant.

WHAT THIS TOOL DECIDES
From one home city, its monthly take-home pay and costs, how every other city compares: the net
salary each city needs for the same lifestyle and savings, what the user's own salary saves in each
city, or what the same multiple of local pay saves there.

   option is better or what the tool will show.
2. Your whole reply is the link and the assumptions behind it, in this order:
   a. a numbered list of every input you chose that the user did not state exactly, each with its

   b. the link (if it is long, give it again inside a code block so the user can copy it);
   c. the sentence: Open the link to see the result.
   Nothing else. No summary, no preview, no figures other than the input values in the link.
3. Build the link in the format below, using only the keys listed here. Start from the nearest

4. Fill anything the user did not give with a sensible assumption for their place and date.
   Prefer what the user said earlier in the conversation over general figures.
5. If you cannot build a link (the request is outside this tool, or you could not read this

LINK FORMAT
https://tool.adjiebrotots.com/costofliving-comparator/map/#load&v=1&qs=<quick start>&<key>=<value>&...&why.<key>=<your reason>&note=<one line>
- After #load come key=value pairs joined by &. Percent-encode each value (a space is %20, & is %26,
  # is %23, + is %2B).
- v=1 is the format version. qs=<key> starts from that Quick Start; without it the tool starts from
  its defaults.
- Then each key in the link is set, in the units its label shows on screen. Numbers may carry $,
  commas and a trailing %. A checkbox takes true or false. A select or a choice takes the value
  listed, or its visible label.
- Anything the link does not name keeps the value it started from, so send only what differs.
- why.<key>=<text>: your reason for that value, shown to the user beside it (200 characters at
  most). note=<text>: one line shown above the list, for example the place and the date.
- A number outside a field's range is pulled into range and the user is told; a key the tool does
  not know is skipped and listed. Never use the keys s, open or embed.

FIELDS (key: label on the page)
Most important: cities, salary, expense, detail, rows, salaryMode, target.

[You]
salary: Net salary  (most important)
  Number (¤ before it, /mo after it). 0 to 100,000,000,000. Default blank.
  The user's monthly take-home pay in the home city, after tax, in the home city's currency (the
  currency tag beside the field shows it). There is no tax model, so convert a yearly or gross
  figure to a monthly after-tax estimate and say so in why.salary. Use the user's own figure when
  they give one. The Scaled salary mode compares it with local pay, so give take-home pay there too.
  Tip on the page: Take-home pay after income tax, a month. Nothing here works out tax.

[Lifestyle]
expense: Living cost  (most important)
  Number (¤ before it, /mo after it). 0 to 100,000,000,000. Default blank.
  Simple lifestyle only (detail=simple): everything the user spends in a month in the home city, in

  Tip on the page: Everything you spend in your city in a month. Each city's figure is this, scaled
  by its cost-of-living index.

COMPACT LISTS
- `cities=<home city>`
- `detail=<simple|detailed>`
- `housing=<include|exclude>`
- `salaryMode=<required|global|scaled>`
- `target=<ratio|nominal>`
- `scale=<min|avg|median>`
- `rows=<category>:<amount>,...`

salary and expense are form fields, listed above; the rest of the plan has no field, so these keys carry it. Amounts are plain numbers (7000, 25000000), with no thousands separators, and always per month, in the home city's currency. A value may also be the words the page shows (Detailed, Global). Anything a link does not name keeps what it started from (the Quick Start, or the page's own start). Which view the map shows and the major-cities filter belong to the reader and are not set by a link.

cities: the home city, matched to the page's own list of cities by name, ignoring case and accents. Use the usual English name. A US or Canadian city may carry its state (Austin, TX or just Austin). Three names are in the list more than once: London (United Kingdom, Canada), Hamilton (Bermuda, New Zealand, Canada) and Kingston (Canada, Jamaica). Write them with the country, as in London, United Kingdom; the country may follow any other city too (Perth, Australia). The map has one home city, so only the first name is used. A name the list does not have is shown to the user as not recognised and the home city is left as it was, and a name that fits more than one city is refused: never invent, translate or swap in a nearby city, and tell the user if their city may be missing.

detail: simple takes one expense total (expense, with housing). detailed takes expense rows (rows). Sending rows switches to Detailed unless detail is sent.

housing: Simple only. include counts rent or a mortgage in the expense total, exclude leaves housing out, as when an employer pays for it. In Detailed there is a rent row instead.

salaryMode: what the map compares. required (the default): the net salary each city needs for the user's lifestyle and savings target. global: the user's own salary in every city at today's exchange rate, and what it leaves after the same lifestyle. scaled: the user's salary as a multiple of their city's pay (see scale), given as the same multiple of each city's pay.

target: Required salary mode only. ratio (the default) matches the share of pay the user saves now in every city; nominal matches the amount saved, at today's exchange rate.

scale: Scaled salary mode only: the local pay the salary is a multiple of. min is the minimum wage, avg the average net salary (the default), median the median net salary.

rows: Detailed expense rows, replacing the Quick Start's rows whole. Items are separated by `,`, the category and the amount by `:`. Categories: rent, groceries, eating_out, utilities, electricity, water, gas_util, fuel, medical, health_insurance, remittance, intl_travel, other. The amount is per month, in the home currency. At most 20 rows. Leave out what the user does not pay (no fuel without a car). utilities is a blend of electricity, water and gas, so do not add those three as well; other holds everything else. Each category is scaled by its own cost index, so a rough row is better than a row put under the wrong category.
`rows=rent:2400,groceries:600,eating_out:400,utilities:250,fuel:250,other:650`
