How to use Finance vs Cash

Pay cash, or borrow and keep your cash earning? This guide shows how to set up a purchase and its loan offers, then how the tool decides. Every demo on this page is the real tool, running live in your browser. Press Pause on one to try it yourself.

  1. 1Load or enter a purchase

    Start from a worked comparison in Quick Start, or enter the cash price, the cash you have, and the Risk-Free Rate your spare cash earns.

  2. 2Describe each loan offer

Quick Start

Six worked comparisons, each a decision people really face: a House, fixed vs float, a Car, 3yr or balloon, a Phone on a plan that quotes no rate, a Credit card 0% conversion, a Motorbike, flat rate, and Furniture, pay later. One click fills every tab and runs it.

On a phone, press Full screen under a demo and turn the phone sideways to read it.

  1. Open the inputs

    On a first visit the card is folded to a rail, so the answer has the room. Click Inputs to open it; it stays the way you leave it.

  2. Pick a comparison

    Hover a button to read what it holds and what it teaches. A click loads it and runs it.

  3. Read the answer

    The verdict and Net Benefit vs Cash: for the car, every loan trails paying cash, the three-year one by the least.

  • A Quick Start is also the reset. Each one is built from the same defaults, not from what is on screen, so it clears the plan before it.
  • Each one carries a lesson. The car's smallest instalment (the balloon) finishes last; the motorbike's 0.9% a month flat costs 21.03% a year; the furniture's payment holiday grows the debt.

The purchase

The Purchase tab holds what both paths share: the price, the cash you have today, and the symbol money is shown in. Typing never reruns the answer; ▶ Simulate does.

  1. The price and your cash

    Cash Purchase Cost is what paying in full costs. Available Cash is what you hold today; it must cover the price.

  2. A price per offer

    Price Each Loan Offer Separately gives each offer its own price, as an amount, a % of cash or a % off.

  3. Simulate

    An edit sends the board out of date and says so above the button. ▶ Simulate runs every offer against cash.

  • Cash below the price is not modelled. The tool compares financing against genuinely paying cash, so the board stays blank and says why.
  • The currency is a label. Nothing is converted.

Loan offers

Each offer is a tab on the bar. The form under it is that offer, in titled sections: Loan Structure, Repayment Term, Interest Rate (or Repayment Plan for a known repayment), then Upfront & Fees. Every edit applies as you make it.

  1. One tab per offer

    Click a tab to edit it, double-click to rename it, click its dot to recolour it. The icons beside the bar duplicate or remove the one on screen.

  2. Add an offer

    + adds one: an amortizing loan at 5% over 60 months, 0% down.

  3. Its loan type

    Loan Type picks one of seven structures. The fields below change to suit it.

  4. Term, rate and fees

    Term counts repayments, Finance Rate is yearly, then the Down Payment, Origination Fee, Fee Treatment and Admin Fee.

  5. Simulate

    The new offer joins the comparison. A 4.9% flat rate shows as 9.32% in Effective Rate (APR).

  • Learn more ↗ under Loan Type opens Loan Types Explained: each structure with its formula, the markets it is sold in and a worked example.
  • Repayment Frequency converts the term when it changes: 60 months becomes 260 weeks, the same five years.

Loan types with a field of their own

Some structures bring a field: Interest-Only or Payment Holiday periods, a Residual, a Repayment Amount instead of a rate. And any rate that can move takes a Rate Basis of Schedule.

  1. Known repayment

    For a plan that quotes an instalment and no rate: enter the Repayment Amount, one figure or a stepped Schedule.

  2. The rate it implies

    Implied Rate is the yearly rate that repays the loan exactly. 24 payments of $82 on $1,800 is 9.08%; of $78, 3.86%.

  3. Fixed, then floating

    A Rate Schedule is consecutive periods, each Fixed or Floating between a min and a max. Here: two years at 5.80%, then 5% to 8.5%.

  4. The band

    A floating rate runs three times, at its min, middle and max. The chart shades the range; the tables use the middle.

  • A flat rate and a known repayment take no schedule. A flat loan fixes its interest at the start; a known repayment has the rate as its answer.
  • No rate repays it? A repayment too small for any rate is skipped, and the banner says to raise it or shorten the term.

Assumptions

What your spare cash earns, how a quoted loan rate is read, and how fast prices rise. The first is the yardstick every offer is measured against.

  1. Risk-Free Rate

    What cash earns in a safe place, such as a savings account. Every offer and the cash purchase use it.

  2. Rate Convention

    Effective annual (EAR) compounds a loan rate down to each repayment; Nominal annual (APR) divides it. Loans only, never the cash rate.

  3. Inflation

    Never changes a loan or the cash. The note under it says what the price costs at the end.

  4. Show in today's money

    Beside the results: every figure divided by inflation up to its own date. Off again, every figure is back to the cent.

The answer

The verdict names the best strategy and by how much it beats, or trails, paying cash. The four figures under it say the same in numbers, and the Scenario Comparison shows how each offer got there.

  1. The verdict

    The best strategy, its Net Benefit vs Cash and when, and its effective rate against the Risk-Free Rate. Here the 5% loan beats cash by $1.5k.

  2. Four figures

    Net Benefit vs Cash: above zero, financing wins. Cash Purchase Wealth: the cash left after paying in full, grown at the risk-free rate.

  3. Optimization Target

    Which offer counts as best: Highest Net Benefit, Lowest Total Interest or Lowest Financing Cost. The 7% loan pays $1,059 less interest, yet trails cash by $842.

  4. Scenario Comparison

    Every figure per offer, each at the end of its own term, against cash over the same time.

  • Banners say what to doubt. Negative carry: a loan's effective rate is above the risk-free rate. Negative amortization: a repayment is below the interest due, so the balance grows.
  • What this assumes, the last card, lists what the answer rests on for this plan: the cash, the rates, the fees, the horizon.

Part 2

How the numbers work

What each field means, where it enters the calculation, and what moving it does to the answer. Every formula here is the one the tool runs.

The model in one picture

Both paths start from the same cash. Paying cash spends the price today and leaves the rest earning the risk-free rate. Financing spends only the down payment and any upfront fee today, keeps the rest earning the same rate, and pays each repayment out of it. The tool compares what each path holds at the end of the offer's term, once the loan is repaid.

Financing W0 = A − D − F0,   Wk = Wk−1 × (1 + i) − Rk − aeach repayment period
Paying cash Wcash = (A − C) × (1 + i)n
Net Benefit vs Cash = Wn − Wcash = (1 + i)n × [ (C − D − F0) − Σ Rk + a(1 + i)k ]
  • AAvailable Cash
  • CCash Purchase Cost
  • Dthe down payment, a share of the offer's price
  • F0an origination fee Paid upfront from cash (else 0)
  • Rkrepayment k of n, set by the loan type
  • athe Admin Fee on every repayment
  • ithe Risk-Free Rate per period: (1 + rf)1/m − 1, with m repayments a year
  • nthe number of repayments, the Term
  • Read the bracket as a present value. It is what financing lets you keep today, less what everything you pay later is worth today at the rate your cash earns. The tool carries that forward to the end of the term, so Net Benefit is in the money of that date.
  • The wash. Borrow at exactly the rate your cash earns, with no fee, and the repayments are worth exactly the loan: Net Benefit is zero for every loan type, frequency, term and down payment. Measured on the car's three-year loan at 4.5% with its fee removed: $0.00.
  • So the rule is one comparison. With no fees and the offer at the cash price, financing wins exactly when the loan's Effective Rate (APR) is below the Risk-Free Rate. Fees count against it, and a cheaper offer price counts for it.
  • Timing. The cash grows for a period first, then the repayment comes out of it. The first repayment falls one period after the purchase, and the last one clears the loan exactly.
  • Each offer is scored at the end of its own term, against paying cash over the same time. Best Strategy is the top offer by the Optimization Target, or Cash Purchase when every Net Benefit is below zero.

What moves the answer

The same loan, with one input moved down and then up and the rest left as the Quick Start sets them. Measured on the tool itself.

  • The spread decides the sign; the term and the amount decide the size. Term tops the chart because it is how long a loan costing 3.9 points more than cash runs. The finance rate and the risk-free rate move the answer almost equally and in opposite directions: only the gap between them counts.
  • No single plausible move rescues this loan. Each bar is at most about $1,000 against a $3,059 loss. It draws level with paying cash only at a Finance Rate of about 3.81%, below the 4.5% risk-free rate because the $400 fee must be earned back too.
  • The cash in the bank does nothing. Its bar is empty: the surplus earns the same either way.
  • For your own decision: argue about the rates first, then the term. If your loan's effective rate is well above what your cash safely earns, a shorter term or a bigger down payment loses less, but paying cash still wins.

What it leaves out

The comparison is clean because it holds a lot still. Know what that is before you act on it.

  • The item itself. It costs the same either way, so what it is later worth is left out of both paths. A car that is worth less on a loan (insurance, a lien) is not modelled.
  • Tax. The cash earns the risk-free rate untaxed, and loan interest is never deductible. If yours is taxed, enter the after-tax rate.
  • Risk and access to cash. The kept cash is assumed safe and earning a fixed rate. Keeping a buffer for an emergency has a value the tool does not count.
  • A rate that floats is three paths, not a forecast. The tables read the middle. Your loan could follow any path inside the band, or outside it.
  • Penalties and promotions. No late fees, early repayment, break costs, or deferred interest charged back when a 0% promotion ends. Only the fees you enter.
  • Different end dates. Each offer is scored in the money of its own end date. Offers of different lengths compare more fairly with Show in today's money on, and that reading uses inflation, not the risk-free rate.
  • Cash that runs out. If the repayments drain the kept cash below zero, the shortfall is charged the risk-free rate, as if you could borrow at it. With Negative carry on a long loan, check the Investment Value line stays above zero.

When not to trust it. If the answer is within a few hundred dollars of zero, the choice of risk-free rate decides it, so test a point either side. If you would invest the kept cash in something risky, the tool's answer is the safe case only. And if the loan has terms you could not enter, such as a promotion that ends, read the contract, not the chart.
