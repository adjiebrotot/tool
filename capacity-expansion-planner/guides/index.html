How to use the Capacity Expansion Planner

Describe a growing demand and what could meet it, then read which plants and batteries to build, how big, and in which year. Every demo on this page is the real tool, running live in your browser. Press Pause on one to try it yourself.

Open the tool Start with the screen

The short version

  1. 1Describe the demand

    The peak now and how fast it grows, the climate and the shape of a day, and the years to plan.

  2. 2Set the supply and the rules

    What is in service and when it retires, what can be built at what cost, then a carbon price, a net-zero year and the discount rate.

  3. 3Run and read

    Press Run plan. The answer says what to build first and when; the charts follow capacity, energy and cost year by year.

Nothing is uploaded. The plan is built by PSA.js, a JavaScript port of PyPSA, and solved by HiGHS, both in your browser.

The screen

Inputs on the left, the answer beside them, the charts and tables under it. Point at a part in the list to see where it is.

  1. Inputs

    Quick Start, save and open, then four tabs: Demand, Supply, Policy and Assumptions. Run plan sits at the bottom. The chevrons fold the card to a slim Inputs rail.

  2. The answer

    What to build first and when, in a sentence, then four figures: new capacity, present cost, LCOE and the clean share of energy.

  3. The charts

    Capacity and when to build, Energy by source with a decision year's typical days under it, and Cost by year. Then the tables and What this assumes.

Top right, Tour replays a one minute walk through the tool, and Guide opens this page.

Quick Start

Four worked plans: a Growing grid whose coal closes, the same grid to Net zero, a Coal exit on a winter-peaking grid, and a tropical Island on diesel. One click replaces the whole plan and runs it.

On a phone, press Full screen under a demo and turn the phone sideways to read it.

  1. Open the inputs

    On a first visit the card is folded to a rail, so the answer has the room. Click Inputs to open it; it stays the way you leave it.

  2. Pick an example

    Hover a button to read what it holds. A click loads it and solves it.

  • Then make it yours. Change anything over the top of an example and run it again.

Demand

How much power is needed now, how it grows, and how it moves through a day and a year.

  1. Peak and growth

    The highest hour of demand in the first year, and how much it grows each year, compounded.

  2. Climate and profile

    The Climate sets the seasons, in either hemisphere. The Profile shapes a day; drag its bars to draw your own.

  3. Planning years

    The first and last year, and how often the plan may build. Each decision year stands for the years up to the next.

  • The first year is also the price year. Every cost you enter and every result is at its prices.
  • Your own hours. Custom takes the profile from a CSV or Excel file: typical days, by season if you like, or a full year.
  • Daily variability. Lets a weekday differ from the weekend, or each day from the next.

Supply

One card per technology: what is in service and when it retires, and whether more can be built and at what cost.

  1. In service and to build

    Each card's line sums it up: 700 MW to 2036, new at $1,000/kW.

  2. Open a technology

    Its existing capacity and retirement year, whether it can build more, its capital cost, and how it runs: a profile and capacity factor for solar and wind, a running cost and emissions for a firm plant.

  3. Detailed

    Adds fixed O&M, lifetime, cost change a year, earliest year, a cap on what may be in service, unit sizes and availability.

  4. Add a technology

    + Add technology offers solar, wind, gas, coal, nuclear, geothermal, diesel, batteries and pumped hydro, with typical costs to start from.

  • Duplicate or remove. The icons on each card's head.
  • Unit sizes make the plan build in whole units, a mixed-integer problem that takes longer to solve.

Policy and run

Reliability and emissions, then Run plan to see what they change.

  1. Reliability

    Whether demand may go unserved, at the value of lost load, when that is cheaper than building for it.

  2. Carbon price

    Paid on every tonne a plant emits, in the first year, and how fast it grows.

  3. Net-zero year

    From this decision year on, no plant may emit CO2.

  4. Run the plan

    Run plan, or Ctrl Enter. Until you do, the last result stays, faded.

Assumptions

The guesses every plan rests on, and how each year is sampled.

  1. Discount rate

    Real, above inflation, or Nominal with inflation, from which the real rate is worked out.

  2. Typical days

    Each year runs over typical days, one or more a season, set by the profiles. A still, overcast peak day makes the plan hold firm capacity for the worst case.

  3. Solver

    A time limit for HiGHS, and with unit sizes an optimality gap.

The answer and the charts

The answer comes first, in words; the charts show how it gets there, year by year.

  1. The answer

    What to build first and when, the year that builds the most, what retires, and the present cost and LCOE at first-year prices.

  2. Capacity and when to build

    What is in service each year, hatched for the plants there now, with each decision year's new build on top. Decision years are in bold.

  3. Click a year

    Opens Typical days for that year: each hour's dispatch, the battery's charging and any curtailment.

  4. Energy and cost

    Where each year's energy comes from, and what each year costs: capital, fixed and running, with the levelised cost on top.

  • Hover any chart to read a year or an hour; ⟳ resets its zoom.

Results by year

The figures behind the charts, a row per year.

  1. Open the tables

    Results by year opens from its chevron.

  2. Pick a table

    Capacity, New build, Energy, Costs or Years. ⬇ CSV saves the one shown.

  • What this assumes. The last card lists what the plan rests on: its demand, its rates and its prices.

Save, export and share

The tool keeps your plan in this browser between visits. The rest makes something to keep or send.

  1. Save or open a file

    The two icons beside Quick Start write the whole plan to a file, and read one back.

  2. Share a chart

    Each chart's Share: SVG or PNG, a copy to the clipboard, a link that opens your plan, or an embed for a web page.
