How to use the Financial Freedom Calculator

How big a pot you need to stop working, and the age your investment first gets there. Every demo on this page is the real tool, running live in your browser. Press Pause on one to try it yourself.

Open the tool How the numbers work

The short version

  1. 1Load or enter a plan

    Start from a worked plan in Quick Start, or enter your ages, what you save or earn, and what you spend now and once you stop.

  2. 2Investment and goal

    What you hold and its return and volatility, what the money should do, then inflation and how sure the answer has to be.

  3. 3Simulate and read

    Press Simulate. Path to freedom gives the age you get there; Cashflows tests stopping at any age you drag to.

Nothing is uploaded. Every figure is worked out in your browser, and your plan is kept in this browser only. This guide is general information, not financial advice.

Quick Start

Six worked plans, each built to show one lever: Moderate FIRE (the baseline), Frugal Living, Geoarbitrage, Bali, Fat FIRE, forever, Family legacy and Late start, on a pension. One click fills every tab and runs it.

On a phone, press Full screen under a demo and turn the phone sideways to read it.

  1. Open the inputs

    On a first visit the card is folded to a rail, so the results have the room. Click Inputs to open it; it stays the way you leave it.

  2. Pick a plan

    Hover a button to read who it describes. A click loads every figure and gives the answer straight away.

  • The slider starts on the answer. No plan sets a retirement age: Retire at opens on the plan's own freedom age, rounded up to a whole year.
  • Then make it yours. Change any figure over the top of a plan. A Quick Start always starts from the defaults, so nothing from the last one carries over.

The You tab

The figures you know: the currency, your ages, the money coming in and the money going out. Every amount is in today's money.

  1. Ages

    Age now starts the clock; Life expectancy is how long the money has to last.

  2. Money in

    Savings is what you put away, or switch to Net income and the tool saves the gap after spending. Growth of that amount is your pay rises.

  3. Money out

    Living expenses now, and Retirement expenses once you stop, as a % of living or an amount.

  4. Simulate

    Typing never reruns the answer: the button lights up instead. Press Simulate to run the plan on the form.

  • A week, a month or a year. Each amount takes its own period. Change the period and the figure is restated, so $60,000 a year becomes $5,000 a month.
  • Government or age pension. Simple mode has a pension switch: its start age, its amount, and whether it Rises with inflation.
  • Currency Symbol is display only. Nothing is converted.

Life stages

Set Detail to Detailed under Money in or Money out and the one figure becomes a list of stages, each between two ages: study, a hustle, kids, part-time work, a pension.

  1. Detailed

    Your entered figure becomes the first stage, named and locked. Your own stages follow it in age order.

  2. A stage of income

    Its own amount and growth from its own start, and Still paid after you stop work for a pension or part-time work.

  3. A stage of spending

    From one age to another, as a % of living or an amount. It replaces your spending for those ages.

  4. The timeline

    Under each list, what the stages resolve to, gaps and all, at the age on the slider.

  5. On the chart

    The stages run along the top of the cashflow chart. With both sides staged, pick Income stages or Expense stages.

  • + Add stage starts a stage where the last one ends. A blank start follows the stage above; a blank end runs to your life expectancy.
  • Up to 12 stages a side. A stage of income ending at 120 has no end: it is paid for life.

The Investment tab

What you hold today, and what you expect it to earn and how much it swings.

  1. Invested assets

    What already earns the return. Not the home you live in.

  2. Pick an asset

    A preset fills the return and volatility from long-run history: cash, bonds, US, Australian or global equity, gold.

  3. Return and volatility

    Expected return (CAGR) is after fees and before inflation. Volatility is how far a year can swing.

  4. A real ticker

    Type one (SPY, VAS.AX) and press Fetch to measure both figures from its real prices.

  • Every field stays editable. A preset is a starting point, not a forecast.

The Goal tab

What should the money do? Three answers, and each needs a different pot.

  1. Just Die

    The pot runs to nothing at your life expectancy. The smallest pot, and no margin if you live longer.

  2. Leave a Legacy

    A set amount, in today's money, is still there at your life expectancy. Enter it under Amount to leave behind.

  3. Die Rich

    Spend only the real growth, so the pot lasts for ever. It needs a return above inflation.

The Assumptions tab

The guesses nobody knows for sure: what prices do, and how sure the answer has to be.

  1. Inflation

    The note under it puts a price on it: what today's spending costs at the age you stop and at your life expectancy.

  2. Confidence level

    The share of futures the confidence pot has to last through. It shows under Chance it works, at the slider's age.

  3. Investment outcome chance

    The investment line is one reached in at least this share of futures. 50% is the expected return; 90% is cautious, and the freedom age moves out.

Path to freedom

One question: how early could you stop? Nothing on this board depends on the retirement slider.

  1. The answer

    Financially free at an age, the Pot needed then, and your Real return, the figure that sizes the pot.

  2. The chart

    Red is the pot needed to stop at each age, blue what your investment grows to, the band the worst and best 10% of futures. Where blue meets red, you are free.

  3. Today's money

    Show in today's money, on by default. Off, each figure reads in the money of its own year, as the account will.

  • Money deposited, the dotted line, is what you have paid in that is still in the pot. Above it is growth.
  • Hover to read. The box under the chart gives any year's figures. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan; ⟳ resets it.

Cashflows

What happens if you stop at this age. The slider is the one control that runs the answer on its own.

  1. Retire at

    Drag the age you stop earning and start living off the pot. Click the age to type one.

  2. The verdict and the cards

    Whether that age works, then the Pot needed, the Balance you stop with, the Chance it works and what is Left at your life expectancy.

  3. Cashflows and balance

    Income against spending above, green while you save and red once the pot pays; the balance under it, with its band of futures.

  • A miss is drawn below zero. A plan that runs out shows a negative balance: the size of the shortfall.
  • Not by this age? The verdict lists the one change that would close the gap: save more, spend less, a higher return, or retire later.

Year by year, and what it assumes

Under the chart, the plan as a cash flow statement; at the foot, the assumptions in figures.

  1. Year by year

    Opens from its chevron. Each row adds up: balance, plus what was saved, plus growth, is the next balance. ⬇ CSV saves it either way.

  2. What this assumes

    Your return, inflation, savings, spending and goal in figures, and the safe withdrawal rate your pot implies.

Save, export and share

The tool keeps your plan in this browser between visits. The rest makes something to keep or send.

  1. Save or open a file

    The two icons beside Quick Start write the whole plan to a file, and read one back.

  2. Share

    Each chart as SVG or PNG, a copy to the clipboard, a link that opens your plan, or an embed for a web page.

  • An embed is live. It shows the chart as you drew it, and a reader can hover and zoom it.
Part 2

How the numbers work

What each field means, where it enters the calculation, and what moving it does to the age you are free. Every formula here is the one the tool runs; its own source opens with the full specification.

The model in one picture

Two curves over age. One is what your investment has grown to if you never stop paying in. The other is the pot you would need if you stopped at that age. The tool's answer is where the first catches the second.

Freedom age = the first a with Wc(a) ≥ Pot(a)
  • aan age, tried month by month from today
  • Wc(a)what the investment has grown to by that age, never drawn on, reached in at least c percent of futures
  • Pot(a)the pot that funds the rest of your life from that age, for your goal
  • cthe Investment outcome chance: at 50% the line is the expected return's
Real return rr = 1 + r1 + i − 1
Monthly rm = (1 + rr)1/12 − 1
  • rthe Expected return (CAGR), before inflation
  • ithe Inflation rate
  • rrthe real return a year, by Fisher rather than subtraction
  • rmthe real return a month, which every step compounds
  • Everything runs in today's money, month by month. Your spending is entered in today's money and stays flat in real terms, so inflation never has to be added back to it; it lives in the real return instead. A figure in the money of the day is only made for display: today's figure × (1 + i)y.
  • Why the pot falls with age. Each year you wait is a year fewer to fund, so the red line drifts down while the blue line climbs. In Simple mode they cross at most once.
  • The retirement slider is a separate question. Neither curve depends on it. It asks what happens if you stop at one age: the Cashflows board, further down.

  • The return is the lever with two ends. A higher return fills the pot faster and makes the pot needed smaller, because the money still invested in retirement keeps earning it.

Watch out. The return you enter is nominal. With 8.5% and 2.5% inflation the real return is 1.085 ÷ 1.025 − 1 = 5.85%, not 6%, and it is the real return that sizes the pot.

Goal: the pot needed to stop

Once you stop, each month's spending comes out at the start of the month and the rest earns the return. The pot needed is the smallest that never goes below zero and meets your goal at the end. With flat spending and no pension that is a textbook annuity.

Just Die Pot = Xr × 1 − (1 + rm)−nrm × (1 + rm)
Leave a Legacy Pot = the Just Die pot + L(1 + rm)n
Die Rich Pot = Xr × 1 + rmrm
  • Xrretirement spending a month, less any pension or income still paid
  • nmonths from the age you stop to your life expectancy
  • Just Die is an annuity paid in advance; Die Rich a perpetuity, which needs rm above 0
  • Lthe Amount to leave behind, in today's money
  • One pass for any plan. With a pension, stages or a draw that changes, each month adds a "never below zero" bound and the goal adds one at the end; the pot needed is the largest of them. Die Rich asks that the pot at age 120 could go on for ever on the draw at that age.

Assumptions: inflation, and how sure the answer is

A single average return hides the order the good and bad years come in. The tool works out the spread of futures instead, in one pass rather than by simulating thousands of them.

A month's real growth = eμ + sz
μ = ln(1 + rr) ÷ 12, s = σ × √(1/12)
  • za standard normal draw, independent month to month
  • μthe drift: the CAGR, so a lump sum's middle future is the expected line
  • σthe Volatility
  • So returns are lognormal. A balance is many deposits, each grown over a different stretch, and that sum has no formula of its own. The tool reads each horizon through one normal factor that weighs every month by the money at stake in it (the comonotonic approximation of Kaas, Dhaene and Goovaerts, 2000), and takes each deposit's exact average given that factor.
  • Worked out, not simulated. A percentile q is that factor at Φ−1(q): no random numbers, the same answer every run, exact for a single lump sum. Against 5,000 simulated futures on every Quick Start, the 90% confidence pot agrees to about 1% and the chance it works to about a percentage point.
Investment line Wc(t) = min( W(t), W1 − c(t) )
Chance it works = Φ(−z*)
  • W(t)the expected-return balance
  • W1 − c(t)the balance at percentile 1 − c of futures
  • Φthe standard normal distribution
  • z*the score at which the pot needed equals the balance you stop with

Moderate FIRE at 45: $1.97m at 90%, $2.36m at 95%. The freedom age does not move.
  • Why a higher chance pushes the age out. At 50% the line is the middle of the road: half of futures do worse. At 90% only one future in ten does worse, so the line sits lower and takes longer to reach the pot.
  • Why the swing of an average narrows. Over many years good and bad months partly cancel, so the band is not one rate run for every horizon: each horizon has its own factor.
  • Sequence risk. A month weighs by the money exposed in it, so a crash in the years either side of stopping counts most: the risk an average return hides.

Watch out. At the default 50% outcome chance, volatility does not move the freedom age at all: that age is read off the expected-return line. Moderate FIRE stopping on it at 45 has a 53% Chance it works, close to a coin flip. Read that card, or raise the outcome chance, before you plan around the age.

What moves the answer most

Measured on the tool's own engine: Moderate FIRE as the Quick Start loads it, each input moved down and up one step while the rest stay put. Left of the line is an earlier freedom age.

  • The real return decides the most. Inflation and the return are the two figures nobody controls, and together they set the real return. One point either way moves the age two to three years. Test your plan at a real return a point lower before you trust it.
  • Your own levers move it about a year for 10%. Spending 10% less does a little more than saving 10% more, because it shrinks the pot for every year of retirement. Under Net income a spending cut does both jobs and moves it nearly twice as far.
  • Risk only shows once you ask for it. At 50% the age ignores volatility; at 90% each 5 points of volatility is about two years. The goal matters less than it sounds: Leave a Legacy with $500,000 is free at 44y 8m, Die Rich at 45y 1m.

Watch it move

The top of the tornado, live: inflation up a point, then down a point, on Moderate FIRE.

  1. Raise inflation

    3.5% on the same 8.5% return: the real return falls to 4.83%, the red line lifts, the blue line flattens. Free at 47y 1m.

  2. Lower it

    1.5%: a 6.90% real return, a smaller pot, a steeper climb. Free at 42y 3m.

  3. Back to the base case

    2.5%, and the answer returns to 44y 4m. Nothing is random, so the same plan always gives the same answer.

What it leaves out

The tool's own What this assumes card lists the assumptions your plan rests on. The ones that matter most:

  • No tax. The return and the savings or income you enter are taken as after tax. Tax differs too much between countries and between ordinary and pension accounts to model honestly in one tool.
  • No fees beyond the return. Enter the return after fees.
  • One return, one inflation rate, for life. Volatility spreads the futures around them, but the long-run average never changes, and one rate covers prices at home and abroad (Geoarbitrage runs 3% for that reason).
  • Returns are lognormal and independent month to month. Real markets have fatter tails and runs of bad years, so the worst futures can be worse than the band.
  • Once you stop, the pot is drawn at the expected return. The outcome chance shapes the years you save; the spread in retirement shows in Chance it works and the band, not in the balance line.
  • No lumpy spending. A stage changes spending for whole months at a time, never for one bill such as a car or a wedding.
  • Spending stays flat in real terms within a stage. Slower later years are a stage you add, not something the tool assumes.
  • No home. Invested assets exclude the home you live in, and no house is bought or sold.

When not to trust it. If your answer only works on the expected return, stopping there is close to a coin flip. If a point less real return pushes it out by years, the plan is thin. And if tax on your savings or withdrawals is large, enter after-tax figures or treat the age as optimistic.
